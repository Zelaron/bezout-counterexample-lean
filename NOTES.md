# Mathematical notes from the formalization

These are observations made while removing the hypothesis on Proposition 4.6 (now proved:
`Principalization.principalizationExtension`). They are not formalized unless a Lean name is
given.

## 1. Only coprime pairs are needed (formalized)

Construction 5.1 applies Proposition 4.6 only to ideals `(a, b)`. In a UFD,
`(a, b) = h·(a', b')` with `a', b'` coprime, and if `(a', b')` becomes principal so does `(a, b)`.
So Theorem 1.1 follows from Proposition 4.6 for ideals generated by two coprime nonzero elements:
`main_theorem_of_coprimePair (hPE : CoprimePairPE)`; `PrincipalizationExtension.coprimePair`
shows this hypothesis is implied by the paper's Proposition 4.6, which is now proved, so
`coprimePairPE_holds : CoprimePairPE` and `main_theorem` are unconditional.

## 2. Unconditional cases of Proposition 4.6 (earlier version, not included)

An earlier version of this project proved the following cases of Proposition 4.6 directly. That
code is not included here, since the weighted principalization supersedes it.

* `a, b` coprime and nonzero, `(a, b)` prime, `A/(a, b)` smooth over `ℚ`. The ring
  `U(a,b) = A[c,s,r,d]/(rc+sd-1, cb-da)` is the blowup, the frame torsor and Jouanolou's device in
  one affine ring; it is a smooth UFD, `(a,b)U = gU` with `g = ra + sb`, and the compact set
  `{r = c, s = d, c² + d² = 1, g ≥ 0}` maps monotonically onto `K` (circles over `K ∩ V(a,b)`).
  The dual derivations needed for smoothness come from the split conormal sequence.
* `(a, b)` has no real zero on `K`: invert `a² + b²`.
* These steps compose, and one may first invert any element without real zeros on `K`.

## 3. Why factoriality (and hence principalization with smooth centres) cannot be avoided

*Necessary condition.* Suppose that at some stage `A_n` an invertible ideal `J` has a
non-orientable real line bundle on `K_n`. If `J` became principal at a later stage `A_m`, a
generator would give a nowhere-vanishing section of the pullback bundle on `K_m`, and orientation
descent (Lemma 2.1 applied to the monotone surjection `K_m → K_n`) would orient `J` on `K_n`.
Hence **every invertible ideal of every stage must be orientable along `K_n`**. Factoriality makes
this automatic, and nothing weaker seems to be preserved by the available constructions.

*Example of failure.* The dimension-preserving ring `Z = A[c,d]/(c²+d²-1, cb-da)`
(`g = ac + bd`; equivalently adjoin `√(a²+b²)` and `a/√(a²+b²)`, `b/√(a²+b²)`) makes `(a, b)`
principal, and inside a Pythagorean field it is always a domain containing `A`. But for
`A = ℚ[x,y]`, `(a,b) = (x,y)` one gets `Z = ℚ[g] ⊗ ℚ[c,d]/(c²+d²-1)`, whose invertible ideal
`(c - 1, d)` restricts to the Möbius bundle on the fibre circle. So `(c-1, d)` can never be made
principal later, and the construction dies. The frame torsor with Jouanolou's device,
`Sl = ℚ[c,d,r,s]/(rc+sd-1)`, is factorial and avoids this; the price is the extra dimensions.

Making non-prime or singular pairs principal while keeping factoriality and connected real fibres
leads straight to embedded principalization with smooth centres (Hironaka / BEV, or the weighted
Abramovich–Temkin–Włodarczyk / cobordant Włodarczyk version).

## 4. A weaker topological invariant suffices for the obstruction (Remark 5.4; formalized)

The argument of §5 needs much less than the compact sets `K_n` and the monotone surjections
between them (`Abstract.lean`).

* *Möbius tests.* A test for a stage `B` is a continuous family `γ : T → Spec(B)(ℝ)` of real points
  with `Δ ∘ γ = 0` along which the Möbius line field `im M` is not orientable (`HasMobiusTest`).
  A test rules out `e · (p, q) M (u, v)ᵀ = 1 + Δc` in `B` (`HasMobiusTest.no_unit_value_mod`).
  The weakest test is the whole real locus: a test exists iff the Möbius line field on
  `V(Δ)(ℝ) ⊆ Spec(B)(ℝ)` is not orientable (`hasMobiusTest_iff`).
* *No compatibility between stages.* Theorem 1.1 holds for the direct limit of any directed system
  of domains with injective maps, Bézout exhaustion (every pair of every stage becomes principal
  later) and a test at every stage (`IsMobiusTower.main`). The tests of different stages need not
  be related. Tests descend along the maps of the tower (`HasMobiusTest.comap`), so it is enough to
  have them at cofinally many stages. The paper's compact sets are tests (`hasMobiusTest_of_set`);
  compactness and the monotone surjections are only used to propagate nonorientability from one
  stage to the next (`hasMobiusTest_of_monotone`, Lemma 2.1).
* *Odd loops.* A loop `c : [a, b] → V(Δ)(ℝ)` on which `(x, y)` has odd degree is a test
  (`not_lineFieldOrientable_of_odd_loop`, `HasOddLoop.hasMobiusTest`), and a tower with such a loop
  at every stage satisfies Theorem 1.1 (`isMobiusTower_of_odd_loops`). The degree is intrinsic:
  angle functions exist (`exists_angle_lift`, path lifting for `exp : ℝ → S¹`) and all of them
  have the same winding (`angle_winding_eq`, `hasOddLoop_iff`). The standard circle recovers
  `L₀_nonorientable` (`hasMobiusTest_A₀`).

At a single stage, an odd loop is not weaker than the paper's invariant: its image is a compact
set with nonorientable Möbius bundle (`exists_compact_of_odd_loop`). What is weaker is dropping the
monotone surjections between stages. The weakest usable invariant is nonorientability on all of
`V(Δ)(ℝ)`, at every stage. With loops, one may also invert any element that does not vanish on the
loop, which makes pairs whose zero set misses the loop trivial (invert `a² + b²`). This does not
obviously remove the need for resolution: crossings of the loop with `V(a, b)` can still occur at
singular points, and keeping the crossings generic requires real general-position arguments.

## 5. Construction 5.1 with arbitrary choices (formalized)

`R hPE` is built from one particular sequence of choices: `Classical.choose` in Proposition 4.6
and a fixed enumeration. Since `CoprimePairPE` is a proposition, varying `hPE` does not vary these
choices. The statement for *every* run of Construction 5.1 is `isMobiusTower_of_monotone` (for a
directed system) and `isMobiusTower_of_sequence` (for a sequence of injections `A₀ ↪ A₁ ↪ ⋯`).
The hypotheses are:

* compact sets `K_n` with `K_0 → K₀` and `K_{n+1} → K_n` monotone surjections;
* Bézout exhaustion.

With these, `IsMobiusTower.main` gives all conclusions of Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 6.4. This holds
whatever extensions and compact sets Proposition 4.6 provides, whatever enumeration is used, and
however many pairs are processed per stage. The formalized `R hPE` is an instance
(`R.isMobiusTower`).

Smoothness and factoriality of the stages are not used in these conclusions. They are needed only
to keep the construction going, that is, to apply Proposition 4.6 again (§3 above).

## 6. Proposition 4.6 gives more than stated (Remark 4.7; formalized)

`Principalization.principalizationExtension_strong` (`Principalization/Strong.lean`): the
extension `f : A ↪ A'`, `K' → K` of the formalized principalization also satisfies the following.

* (a) `K' → K` is injective over `K ∖ V(I)(ℝ)` and restricts to a homeomorphism there. In a torsor
  step, the fibre of the weighted sphere bundle over a point not killing the centre `𝔭` is a single
  point (`torsorK_injAway`, from `eq_ptA`).
* (b) `A → A'` is smooth away from `V(I)`. Precisely: `f` is of finite presentation, `A'_P` is
  formally smooth over `A` for every prime `P ⊉ IA'`, and `A → A'_{f(a)}` is smooth for `a ∈ I`.
  In a torsor step, `a ∈ 𝔭` gives `a^j ∈ F_j`, so over `A_a` the Rees algebra is `A_a[T, T⁻¹]`
  (`reesAlg_loc_away_top`), and the torsor is a Jouanolou device over it
  (`torsor_formallySmooth_away`). Divisorial steps are identities.
* (c) `IA' = tA'` where `t` is a unit times a product of primes `p` with `IA' ⊆ pA'`, the
  exceptional primes. In the UFD `A'` this is automatic once `IA'` is principal.

## 7. Krull dimension and Noetherianity (formalized)

`Dimension.lean` (Proposition 6.2). With `f = 1 + x`, the multiplicative set
`S = {fⁿ (1 - f r)}` does not meet `ΔR`: a relation `fⁿ (1 - f r) = Δ h` would make the row
`(g, 1 - g) M`, `g = f r`, unimodular modulo `Δ` (`exists_unit_value_mod_of_pow_mul`), contrary to
the key obstruction `R.no_unit_value_mod`. A prime `𝔭 ⊇ ΔR` disjoint from `S` and a maximal ideal
`𝔪 ⊇ 𝔭 + fR` give `0 ⊊ 𝔭 ⊊ 𝔪` (`R.exists_prime_lt_maximal`), so `dim R ≥ 2`
(`R.two_le_ringKrullDim`), and `R` is not Noetherian, since a Noetherian Bézout domain is a PID
(`R.not_isNoetherianRing`). Topologically: a relation `fⁿ (1 - f r) = Δ h` in `A_N` would make
`z ↦ M(z) (g(z), 1 - g(z))ᵀ` equal to the first column of `M(z)` where `x(z) ≠ -1` and to the
second column where `x(z) = -1`, a nowhere vanishing section of `L_N` on `K_N`.

Dimension at least two is forced for every Bézout domain that is not an elementary divisor
domain (Remark 6.3): proper quotients of a Bézout domain of dimension `≤ 1` are zero-dimensional,
hence of stable range one, and such Bézout rings are elementary divisor rings (McGovern, JPAA 212
(2008), Theorem 3.7). This is not formalized. The exact Krull dimension of `R` is unknown. The
argument uses only the Möbius obstruction. For `R hPE`, whose stages are only known to satisfy the
conclusion of Proposition 4.6 (which allows `K_{n+1} → K_n` to be homeomorphisms), it is not clear
how the real points could give a better bound.
